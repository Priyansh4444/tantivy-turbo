A's optional-only predicate is a valid smaller implementation of equality: proposed certificates are at least the original tight effective certificates; the old rank k remains covered by its original selected bound; original-n sequential nonnegative sums and bound transform are monotone. A successful proposed prefix[k] cannot move k later, while the old prefix[k+1] already exceeds the threshold. A failed optional prefix therefore indicates promotion. Keeping raw bounds/live prefix unchanged until admission avoids real cursor rollback. Its allowed PendingNative policy initialization means rejection preserves certificate/prefix/partition/cursor semantics, not whole scorer object bytes.

A addresses promotion categorically and preserves batch-versus-scalar membership at that boundary. Its cost limitation is decisive here: a rejected proposal can parse an unbounded metadata span repeatedly at successive tight boundaries. It also adds128 logical bytes of proposed-bound scratch over source13 and conditional commit work. No design evidence bounds that possible residual overhead. Its test plan is particularly strong: actual promotion rejection, virtual-zero rejection, useful nonpromotion acceptance, zero restoration while optional, and always-admit/always-reject counterexamples.

B leaves source13's complete final coverage/repartition intact and uses one endpoint expression. It bounds each newly expanded interval to128 physical addresses and, at the actual prepared caller, at most two overlapping full128-posting blocks per crossed clause. That bound depends on valid sorted distinct postings and the existing shallow selection at max(lo,real first unread); it is not a new bound on arbitrary private helper requests or on whole-query time. A window intersecting three full blocks would need one posting from the first, all128 from the middle and one from the third, exceeding128 addresses. It also bounds distinct candidate docs per expanded region, while leaf/probe work remains proportional to clauses. Promotions, whole boundary-block over-coverage, repeated small proposals, conservative tails and native policy initialization still cost real work.

B preserves already-wide tight sparse regions rather than subdividing them. That is essential: final hi is max(tight_hi,min(old_essential_end,physical_end)), not an unconditional minimum. A tight region spanning509 addresses at lo0 must stay509, not shrink to128. This is the strongest direct structural limit on unbounded added work among these packages; it does not guarantee that sparse queries recover or the aggregate gain survives.
